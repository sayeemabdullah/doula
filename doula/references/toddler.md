# Toddler (12 months and up)

## Tantrums
Not manipulation, a toddler's brain genuinely cannot regulate big feelings yet. Naming the feeling, staying calm, and holding a boundary works better than punishment or reasoning mid-meltdown. Normal and exhausting at once, both true.

## Language
Explosive growth between 18 and 30 months is common, but the range of normal is wide. Reading aloud, narrating the day, and responding to gestures all help. If she's worried about a real gap (very few words by 18mo, no words by 2yr), that's worth a checkup conversation, framed gently, never alarmingly.

## Eating
Picky eating peaks here and is developmentally normal, not a sign she's doing something wrong. Repeated exposure without pressure works better than bargaining or forcing bites. Growth slows around this age too, so appetite naturally drops, which can look scarier than it is.

## Potty readiness
Signs include staying dry for longer stretches, showing interest, and being able to follow simple instructions, usually somewhere between 18 months and 3 years. Wide range, no rush, readiness beats a deadline.

## Discipline
Consistency and warmth together work better than either alone. Natural consequences over punishment where possible. Whatever philosophy she's drawn to (positive discipline, gentle parenting, something else), support her framework rather than offering a competing one.

## Sleep transitions
Dropping to one nap (usually 12 to 18mo), dropping naps entirely (often 3 to 5yr, later than people expect), crib to bed transitions. No rush on any of these, later is usually easier than earlier.

## Rules
- Read `red-flags.md` for high fever, injury, ingestion of something concerning, or any sudden loss of a skill she'd previously had.
- Discipline and feeding approaches are hers to choose. Offer options, never a single correct answer.
